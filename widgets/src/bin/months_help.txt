A month grid you can page through, with today in context.

A calendar in a pane: today marked, at least two weeks of context either side
of it, days belonging to the neighbouring months dimmed, and paging by month
or by year that runs as far as the calendar itself does.

Nothing here comes off the network and nothing needs a credential. Every date
is computed from the system clock and the timezone database, which is the same
source clocks reads.

The month today is in is drawn taller than a month is: a fixed grid cannot
promise two weeks either side of the current week, because today in the top
row has no weeks above it. Leading or trailing weeks are added until it does,
and they read as what they are - days outside the month, dimmed like any other
spill. Months today is not in are drawn as ordinary grids, and the grid area
keeps its height either way so paging does not walk the footer up and down.

Week numbers are ISO 8601, which counts weeks from Monday and gives week 1 to
the week holding the year's first Thursday. That is what the `wk` heading says
on screen, because the grid can start on Sunday instead and then the number
belongs to a week beginning a day earlier - at the turn of a year the two
genuinely disagree, and a number whose meaning moves with a config key is
worse than no number.

Weeks start on Sunday unless "week_start": "monday" says otherwise. Dates are
reckoned on this machine's zone unless "timezone" names another; either way
the zone is on the second line, because "today" is a different date in two
places for several hours a day.

A wide pane shows more months rather than wider margins - two at the sixty to
seventy columns these panes usually get, three from ninety, up to a year
across - and a narrow one drops the week-number gutter before it drops a day.

    months

Keys: left and right page a month, up and down a year, and both run
indefinitely - h/l and j/k do the same. t returns to the month it is now, and
so does Home. q quits.

ctrl-y and ctrl-e scroll the view a line at a time, as in vim, and the mouse
wheel does the same.
