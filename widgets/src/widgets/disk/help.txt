Which filesystems can still take a write, which disks are doing I/O, and, on Linux, whether I/O is stalling.

Space and inodes come from the kernel. Linux reads the mount table and
statvfs; macOS reads getmntinfo. Pseudo filesystems, bind mounts and
read-only mounts are hidden, because none of them can fill. A mount
covered by a later one at the same path is hidden too, since a lookup
there only reaches the one on top. A mount that does not answer within
two seconds stays on screen as having no answer, and the poll keeps going.

DISKS is whole disks only. Linux reads /proc/diskstats (512-byte sectors)
and, once a minute, /sys/block. macOS reads iostat, which reports read and
write combined and does not have await, busy or queue. There the write
sort is combined MB/s and the read sort is tps. Without iostat the
filesystem table still runs. PRESSURE is Linux-only, from /proc/pressure/io.
A kernel without PSI says so. A Mac does not draw that section.

Under the disk table one chart draws read growing up and write growing
down, each to its own peak; on macOS it is one trace of combined I/O.
Until the disk section has focus it is the total of every disk; then it
is the selected disk, named above it.

    disk [-n SECONDS]

Keys: up/down select, enter opens, s cycles the sort of the focused
section, w cycles the filesystem window (6, 12 or 24 hours; not on a
disk's detail), tab moves between sections, v toggles idle disks, p
pauses, esc goes back, comma opens settings, q quits.

Press ! to read this widget's notices - warnings that are not about a number
on screen, such as a config file others can read or a recommended tool that is
missing - and dismiss them for this session, for 7 days or for 30. A new notice
shows under the footer for a minute, and a [!] count stays on the top row for
as long as it is still true. Clicking the count opens the same screen.

ctrl-y and ctrl-e scroll the view a line at a time, as in vim, and the
mouse wheel does the same. pgup, pgdn, home and end move that same view.
They move the pane and never the selection, which is what the arrows are for.
