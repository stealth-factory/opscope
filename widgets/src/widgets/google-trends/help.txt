What a region is searching for right now, from Google's own Trending Now feed.

Reads Google's public Trending Now RSS feed for each configured region - no
account, no key - and shows every trend in Google's order with its traffic
bucket exactly as Google writes it, when it started, and the news Google
attaches. What changed between fetches - new trends, and the ones that left
the feed - is this widget's own observation, and is labelled that way.

    google-trends [-n SECONDS] [GEO ...]

Polls every 300s by default (-n changes it, minimum 60s). Region codes on the
command line replace google_trends.geos for that run.

Keys while running: up/down select a row, tab moves between TRENDING NOW,
LEFT THE FEED and REGIONS, left/right step between regions, Enter opens the
news for the selected trend (or switches to the selected region), / filters
by term, story headline or news source, esc clears the filter. In the list c
copies the trend's Google Trends Explore link; in the news view c copies the
selected story's URL, and esc, left or backspace go back. r refreshes now,
comma opens settings, q quits.

Press ! to read this widget's notices - warnings that are not about a number
on screen, such as a config file others can read - and dismiss them for this
session, for 7 days or for 30. A new notice shows above the footer for a
minute, then as a [!] count in the footer for as long as it is still true.

Copying uses OSC 52, so the terminal you are sitting at performs it. If your
terminal blocks OSC 52, the link is shown on screen instead.

ctrl-y and ctrl-e scroll the view a line at a time, as in vim, and the
mouse wheel does the same. They move the pane and never the selection,
which is what the arrows are for.
